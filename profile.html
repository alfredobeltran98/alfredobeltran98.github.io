02 / Practice

The problems I keep solving

Source-of-truth architecture

One number, one definition. I have rebuilt stacks where three teams reported three different revenue figures and none of them agreed. The fix is rarely a better chart, it is deciding what the number means and enforcing it upstream. In practice that means retiring month-end extracts for a governed layer and the dashboard that runs on it, refreshed daily or faster.

Decision-first reporting

A dashboard is finished when someone can say what they will do differently after looking at it. Getting there is usually a persuasion problem rather than a design one, because the hard part is not building the number, it is getting a room to act on it. I design backwards from the decision and then go argue for it.

Commercial and operational analysis

Pricing, inventory, upsell, supply chain. The analysis lands because I have sat on the operating side of it and know which constraints are real and which ones are habit.

Testing what we assume

Most analytics stops at correlation and files it as a finding. Lift tells you two products get bought together, not that recommending one causes the other to sell. The questions worth answering are the ones a designed experiment settles and an argument does not, and that is the direction I am taking my practice and my master’s.
